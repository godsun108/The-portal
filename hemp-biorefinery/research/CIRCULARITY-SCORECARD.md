## Carbon math discipline
Track plant biogenic carbon per audited cultivation life cycle. Carbon mineralization, energy combustion, char application, product decay and transport all matter. Credit only independently verified durable storage with methodology-specific boundaries and no double-claiming.
- Biochar destined for material conversion is *not* credited as agricultural biochar.
- A biochar-derived carbon-filler product can have durability potential but is not automatically eligible for any credit.
- Soil benefits vary by crop/soil application, local nutrients and conditions; measure against appropriate controls.
- Hemp cultivation can improve certain rotation systems but monoculture may degrade soils; plan regenerative farming criteria and avoid sweeping claims.

## Commercial feasibility metrics
- Sale price minus quality/packing/distribution costs by product stream (no double counting).
- Cost per tonne dry feedstock and fraction-specific yield, energy/water and reagents per tonne.
- Capex by each optional module (decortication, wet fractionation, pyrolysis, refining, materials compounding, graphene R&D).
- Offtake qualification, product insurance and permits.
- Sensitivity to feedstock price, capacity utilization, electricity rates and scale.

## Stop conditions
If biochar fails soil-grade tests: prohibit agricultural sales or application until independently resolved.
If graphene feedstock or product fails technical characterization: market accurately as char/graphitic carbon if actually suitable; do not mislabel.
If lifecycle greenhouse-gas balance is positive or resource use is excessive: abandon carbon-negative claims and reconsider process allocation.
If costs exceed achievable product prices: do not deploy the module.
