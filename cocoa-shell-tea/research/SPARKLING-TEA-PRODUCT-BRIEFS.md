# Sparkling Tea — Three First-Article Briefs
Updated 2026-10-12. **Product hypotheses, not formulas or approved recipes.** Mission: substitute for everyday soda with satisfying carbonation, strong taste and price accessibility.

## Common customer and product criteria
- Buyer: regular soda purchaser curious about tea-forward alternatives; household, convenience, café and grocery occasions to test separately.
- Differentiation hypothesis: brewed-tea identity with soda-level refreshment and less added sugar; test rather than assert consumers prefer it.
- Initial comparison axes: unsweetened vs lightly sweetened vs alternative-sweetened; caffeine/decaf; 12oz can; single and multipack; perceived flavor, repeat desire and willingness to pay.
- Must record sugar, calories, caffeine and ingredient sources accurately; do not promise functional effects.

### Candidate A: Peach Black Tea
Familiar peach aroma, black-tea finish, carbonated soda experience. Major unknowns: tea bitterness after storage, peach authenticity, caffeine level, acceptable sweetness and price.

### Candidate B: Citrus Green Tea
Lemon-lime-forward bright refreshment with green-tea foundation. Unknowns: stability/tea flavor, sweetener finish, acidity and carbonation balance.

### Candidate C: Cherry Hibiscus
Bold fruit/tart botanical flavor intended to compete with cherry soda; tea classification and brewed tea identity must be clarified if hibiscus is primary botanical.

## Bench sensory *study design* (not authorization)
Recruit intended adult users only after legal/safety review of selected conventional food-grade ingredients and safe handling procedures. Blind-coded comparison with known soda benchmarks. Ask overall liking 1–9, sweetness just-right, carbonation just-right, preferred use occasion, expected price and purchase likelihood. Record product identities, weights and response counts; don't fabricate results.
