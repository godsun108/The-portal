# SEB-001-IMP-001 — Implementation Plan

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Stage-gated plan; no spend authorization

## 1. Delivery philosophy
Advance from evidence to pilot validation to modular industrial implementation. Do not commit land, capital, equipment or contracts on the basis of conceptual yields. Maintain separate industrial siting from Sacred Earth Center 001. Preserve optionality through vendor-neutral studies and independently verified decision gates.

## 2. Work breakdown and gates
| Phase | Scope | Exit criteria / decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — Project control | Assign owner, engineering authority, document control, legal entity and risk governance | Approved project charter, document register, change control and capped study budget |
| G1 — Feedstock and market evidence | Supplier mapping, legal chain of custody, whole-stalk and separated-fraction assays, customer specifications | Verified supply envelope and written product requirements |
| G2 — Process selection | Compare cross-current multistage routes and alternatives; lab/pilot partner capability; preliminary PFD and balances | Independently reviewed route selection and measured mass/energy/water data |
| G3 — Site due diligence | Identify candidate Florida industrial parcels near Center 001; zoning, flood, access, utilities, environmental and geotechnical checks | Site scorecard and permit pathway; no land purchase before owner approval |
| G4 — Pre-FEED / FEED decision | PFDs, P&IDs, equipment datasheets, utility balance, HAZID, emissions/wastewater basis, cost estimate | Qualified engineering review and estimate with stated uncertainty |
| G5 — Pilot demonstration | Approved third-party pilot, product QA, safety and environmental monitoring | Reconciled data, product qualification and customer feedback |
| G6 — Stage 1 authorization | 1 dry t/day initial plant package, contracts, financing, permits, construction package | Explicit owner investment decision; all permits and professional approvals |
| G7 — Commissioning / performance | Mechanical completion, pre-startup safety review, training, performance testing | Safe operation, acceptance tests and documented performance |
| G8 — Expansion | 5, 10 then 25 dry t/day modules | Each expansion independently passes safety, market, utility and profitability gates |

## 3. Workstreams
Process engineering; feedstock and agronomy; product/material science; carbon/graphene R&D; site/civil; utilities; HSE/environmental; permitting/legal; procurement/vendor qualification; project controls; financing/partnerships; QA/customer qualification. Interfaces and responsible parties must be assigned in the project execution plan.

## 4. Schedule logic
No calendar duration is promised at this revision. Develop a logic-linked schedule after site, permitting jurisdiction, feedstock test availability, pilot partner lead times, vendor data and financing strategy are known. Long-lead equipment and permit reviews must be identified before any baseline schedule is approved.

## 5. Stage 1 minimum viable scope
The first module should prioritize measured mechanical separation and a validated fractionation route, with laboratory product qualification and compliant waste handling. Avoid bundling speculative commercial fuel production or graphene manufacturing into the first construction scope. Maintain graphene as a separately gated R&D program, and do not represent its future revenue as necessary to justify the base plant.

## 6. Governance and approval limits
This plan does not authorize paid studies, sample shipments, NDAs, land offers, grant/loan applications with binding obligations, equipment deposits, purchase orders, contracts, site works or construction. The owner must approve each expenditure and commitment. Qualified professionals control technical deliverables within their licensed scope.

## 7. Implementation acceptance criteria
A phase closes only when its evidence package, risks, cost, schedule, permits, product criteria, open items and change log are reviewed and accepted. Unresolved safety, environmental, feedstock, mass balance or product-quality gaps prevent progression.
