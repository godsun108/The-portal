# SEB-001-GM-001 — Graphene Research Program

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Research-only; no operating or commercial claim

## 1. Mission and boundary
Evaluate whether selected hemp-derived carbon streams can be upgraded into reproducible graphene-like / graphitic materials for qualified advanced-material applications. The selected owner concept is graphene system C: a contained process with capture, recovery, material accounting and recirculation where technically justified. This is an R&D objective, not a claim that ordinary pyrolysis produces graphene or that a closed-loop process is already proven.

## 2. Scientific distinction
- **Biochar:** heterogeneous carbon-rich solid from thermochemical conversion; composition, porosity, ash and contaminants depend on feed and process.
- **Graphitic / graphene-like carbon:** structurally distinct carbon materials requiring specific synthesis and characterization.
- **Graphene:** a defined nanocarbon structure; do not label a product “graphene” based only on carbon content, color, conductivity or visual appearance.
The biochar-to-graphene literature is proof of principle, not a hemp-specific commercial yield or cost guarantee.

## 3. Research hypotheses
1. Characterize clean hemp-derived char and compare its suitability with other carbon precursors.
2. Compare published upgrading pathways, including flash Joule heating and other specialist methods, through peer-reviewed literature and qualified institutional partners.
3. Determine whether upgrading can produce a target material meeting defined structural, chemical, electrical and dispersion specifications.
4. Design an enclosed capture/recovery concept with measured collection efficiency, losses, recycle streams and occupational exposure controls.
5. Evaluate composite applications only after independent material characterization and comparison with commercial carbon black / graphite / graphene benchmarks.

## 4. Program stages and gates
- **GM-0 — Specification:** choose a first target application and define performance requirements with prospective users.
- **GM-1 — Feed characterization:** analyze feed carbon, ash, metals, PAHs, moisture, particle size and batch variability.
- **GM-2 — Route screening:** literature and institutional partner review; compare technical maturity, energy, off-gas, safety and IP.
- **GM-3 — Approved external experiments:** only with owner approval, qualified lab, written safety protocol, waste plan and budget.
- **GM-4 — Material characterization:** Raman spectroscopy, microscopy, elemental/chemical analysis, layer/thickness distribution, defect structure, surface chemistry, contaminants and batch repeatability as appropriate.
- **GM-5 — Capture and recovery validation:** enclosed handling, measured mass recovery, filter/collector integrity, cleanability, exposure monitoring and waste classification.
- **GM-6 — Application validation:** polymer/composite coupon trials, conductivity/mechanical testing, dispersion, durability, cost and lifecycle comparison.
- **GM-7 — Scale-up decision:** independent TEA, HSE review, permitting and verified customer interest before any industrial module.

## 5. Closed-loop accounting
Track carbon feed entering upgrading, product mass, captured off-spec material, unrecovered loss, residues, off-gas carbon, filter media, wash/recovery media and any recycled fraction. Report gross and net yields on dry, ash-free and elemental-carbon bases where appropriate. Do not count recirculated material as new production. No uncharacterized carbon dust may be released to work areas or environment.

## 6. Analytical acceptance criteria
A batch is not accepted as graphene until independent methods demonstrate that it meets the pre-agreed structural and chemical specification. At minimum, define method, calibration, sample preparation, detection limits, uncertainty and batch sampling. For application qualification, compare against commercial reference materials at equal loading and identical test conditions. Claims of improved strength, conductivity, barrier performance, durability or environmental benefit require statistically meaningful comparative data.

## 7. Safety and environmental controls
All high-current, high-temperature, gas-generating, fine-particle and nanoscale-carbon work is performed only by qualified facilities under approved engineering controls. Require electrical safety review, off-gas characterization, ventilation and source capture, exposure assessment, fire/explosion review, hazardous waste classification and emergency response. No DIY apparatus or open powder collection is within project scope.

## 8. Commercial discipline
No graphene sales, yield, price, market share, carbon credit or revenue is included in the base-case CAPEX/OPEX model. The program advances only if target specifications, reproducibility, energy demand, capture performance, worker protection, customer validation and economics are demonstrated.
