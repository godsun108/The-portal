# Supply resilience and ownership ladder — portfolio operating doctrine
Date: 2026-10-13. Research and future planning only. No purchases, farm investments, contracts, acquisitions, supplier guarantees or regulatory approvals.

## Mission
Never allow a single supplier or manufacturing bottleneck to jeopardize a core product where there is an economically viable alternative. Build redundancy first, then evaluate control through contract farming, partnerships, equity investment or wholly owned farming/manufacturing **when cost, safety and risk-adjusted returns favor it**.

## Tiered sourcing strategy
1. Map **critical inputs**, substitutability, lead time, origin, ownership, power/water/transport dependencies and alternative suppliers. Avoid false redundancy: two vendors buying from the same farm or using the same processing facility are one common-cause risk.
2. Seek primary + independently qualified secondary supplier for critical materials. Track document validity, actual inventory, processing region, lot COAs and recall/traceability.
3. Develop third-party contract manufacturing or dedicated supply partners where customer demand and scale justify; protect intellectual property and maintain alternate supplier rights.
4. Evaluate production capacity reservation and farm grower agreements, but insist on written commercial terms and practical exit conditions; do not bind future cash without owner authorization.
5. Consider minority farm/processor investment, joint ventures or leased capacity only if strategic supply and independent financial return justify the risk.
6. Own farmland and processing facilities after rigorous make-versus-buy modeling, staffing/animal welfare and compliance support, not simply because ownership seems self-sufficient.

## Decision model (per product/material/animal operation)
Compare alternatives under base / shortage / quality failure / high demand / low demand scenarios:
- **Buy:** unit landed price + testing/QA + shipping/storage + working capital + expected shortage/quality loss.
- **Contract:** producer charges + site/capacity fees + QA + logistics + governance + opportunity cost of guaranteed volumes.
- **Invest / joint venture:** cash contributed + dilution/governance + capital calls + credit risk + operating economics + supply rights + exit costs.
- **Own:** cost of land/equipment/animals + cost of capital + labor including unpaid operator labor + care/insurance/permits/water/energy/maintenance + spoilage/waste + taxes + alternative use value + decommissioning. Subtract independently realizable product sales, not aspirational revenue.
- Record NPV/IRR or comparable investment criteria, break-even volume, downside cash need and recoverability of invested funds.
- Score supply resilience separately from profit (two independent economic benefits); avoid counting the same benefit twice.

## Common-cause risk and true redundancy
Both supplier A and B may depend on same importer, harvest region, breed stock, seed genetics, contract processor, power grid or trucking lane. Inventory on a website is not a supply guarantee. Evaluate region, weather, import tariffs, food-safety requirements, labor constraints and transport failure correlations.
For critical inputs, qualify at least two *operationally independent* pathways where feasible. Specialty/regulated materials may only have one source initially: flag critical risk and investigate design substitution or buffer inventory.

## Portfolio examples
- **Cocoa Shell Tea:** Origen, ChocoSol, Mānoa being contacted Oct 13, 2026; Mountain Rose long-term unavailable. None qualified. Seek two sources, independent current-lot COAs, commercial tea use and full costs before purchases.
- **Hemp textiles:** multi-region bast-fiber suppliers, contract spinners, contract weavers and sewing partners. Farmland could supply stalks, but textile quality depends on retting/processing and fiber spinning capacity.
- **Wool/sheep:** consider wool buys from qualified flocks and mills before our own herd; if later owned, price shearing, scouring, lamb care and land per unit of marketable fleece.
- **Cattle:** buying livestock is NOT a prerequisite to keeping livestock product supply secure; farm partnerships can reduce fixed exposure. Feed only species-approved ingredients and veterinarian/nutritionist-approved diets; hemp cattle feed legality remains a separate regulatory gate.
- **Mushroom materials:** two clean biomass suppliers, strain/spawn redundancy and alternative vetted substrate choices; avoid food/industrial chemical cross-contamination.
- **Industrial biorefinery:** qualify biomass feedstock across independent farms and contract processors; choose first products and buyers before owning acreage, reactors or graphene facilities.

## Cattle farm investment / ownership gate
Owner decision: beef (cow-calf vs finishing), dairy, land stewardship or multiple goals. Compare bought cattle products, offtake agreements, leasing pasture to operators, minority investment in an existing farm and direct ownership.
USDA ERS 2024 discussion of historical 2018 ARMS study found $2,099/cow total economic costs on 20–49 cow operations vs $910/cow on 500+ operations; **historical averages, NOT Florida/current/project quotes**. See https://www.ers.usda.gov/amber-waves/2024/december/larger-beef-cow-calf-farms-have-lower-costs-per-cow-than-smaller-operations .
Account for hurricane risk, wet-season parasites, land/water access, grazing capacity, fencing and caring labor. No livestock acquisition without humane care and separately approved budget.

## 2026–27 research sequence (not promised deadline)
Stage 0: critical-input register and alternative source search. Current.
Stage 1: primary/backup written commercial capabilities and samples ONLY when qualified and spending authorized.
Stage 2: compare actual delivered unit cost, quality and service history; secure nonbinding partner relationships.
Stage 3: if revenue is demonstrated, compare contract-grown and contract-manufactured input cost/quality.
Stage 4: if financing and existing operator data are available, independently evaluate farm or processor minority interest, leases and joint venture.
Stage 5: full farm/processing ownership only after capex, operational capability, permits, reliable buyers, downside reserve, return on invested capital and alternative vendor strategies all support it.
