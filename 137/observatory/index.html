01 / The measured constant

Not exactly 137.

Established science
Low-energy inverse fine-structure constant
137.035999

The famous 137 is a memorable approximation. The measured low-energy value is more precise—and the constant itself is dimensionless.

α = e² / (4π ε₀ ℏ c)
Why dimensionless matters

It compares the strength of electromagnetic interaction without depending on our choice of units. That makes it a particularly meaningful number to compare across experiments and theories.

The value shown is rounded for display. Consult CODATA for the recommended value and uncertainty.

04 / A beautiful coincidence?

137.036 meets 137.508°.

Open exploration
Inverse fine-structure constant
137.036

A dimensionless physical coupling written as its inverse.

Golden angle
137.508°

An angle associated with efficient packing patterns in some plant growth arrangements.

The numerical resemblance is intriguing, but these quantities describe different things. No established physical theory derives one from the other. A visual comparison is not evidence of a causal connection.

05 / The frontier

The question remains.

Active research

Why this value?

We can measure the fine-structure constant with extraordinary precision and calculate how it behaves across energy scales. But no experimentally confirmed deeper theory explains why its low-energy value is what it is.

That is the open door: not a solved mystery, but a well-defined question.
