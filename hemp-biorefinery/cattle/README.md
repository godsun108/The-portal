# Regenerative Cattle & Hemp Feed Feasibility Program
Date: 2026-10-12. Research only. **No cattle acquired, no hemp fed, no feed trial, veterinary treatment, pasture lease, food product or spending authorized.**

## Vision
Eventually keep cattle on regenerative pasture while using selected hemp-derived feed components **only when they are specifically lawful and safe for the species, production stage and food end use**. Potential beef, dairy or dual-purpose operations require different animal welfare, milk/meat residue and commercial plans. Make herd size a function of land carrying capacity and animal care, not a fixed aspirational number.

## Urgent regulatory fact
FDA human-food GRAS notices for hulled hemp seeds, hemp oil and seed protein DO NOT approve cattle rations. AAFCO accepted a mechanically extracted hemp seed meal feed ingredient **for laying hens at specified levels**, not an across-species feed clearance. Verify up-to-date FDA/AAFCO definition and Florida feed authorities before using any hemp-derived feed in cattle. No CBD-rich leaves, flower or spent extracted biomass for food animals on assumption that industrial hemp is legal.

## Evidence and animal-food safety
- 2023 USDA-ARS studied hempseed cake in cattle; research was designed to characterize cannabinoid residues, not constitute feed approval.
- 2023 nonlactating cow study (six animals) reported conditional dietary results on hemp meal; too small/limited to establish all-age/dairy-market clearance.
- 2025 study of spent hemp biomass found cannabinoid accumulation in milk and fat and possible exceedance of dietary THC thresholds. Hemp leaf study observed prolonged cannabinoid tissue retention.
- Meat and milk products may create consumer exposure and FDA residue questions even without immediately apparent animal-health effects.
The herd must have veterinary-approved nutritionally adequate non-hemp diets unless lawful and independently reviewed species-specific use is established.

## Multi-stage trajectory (gate-based, not guaranteed dates)
**Phase 0 NOW: research, no animals.** Confirm Florida county zoning/land use, animal husbandry experience, humane stocking and land costs, grazing and water availability, feed eligibility, local meat/dairy sales and economic models. Determine beef vs dairy vs dual-purpose in a separate decision.
**Phase 1: farm design.** Choose climate-appropriate breed(s) based on verified management goals, veterinary and extension advice, heat tolerance and local feed market; identify qualified veterinarian, experienced mentor, pasture/forage plan, fencing, water, shade, shelter, emergency handling equipment and neighbor/waste rules.
**Phase 2: land and finances.** Model land lease/purchase, pasture soil assay, stocking-rate estimate from extension/local carrying capacity (seasonal drought and storm stress included), forage, hay reserves, feed, fencing, water, veterinary care, insurance, transport and emergency cash. Gate: entity/permits/animal welfare and specific budget authorized.
**Phase 3: small conventional-feed herd.** Only when housing, safe water, forage, veterinary services, transport, records and owner labor are prepared; no preset animal count. Rotate pastures as agronomically suitable, monitor body condition and animal health; record true cost per animal and welfare.
**Phase 4: hemp seed product research partner.** If legal approval becomes clear, seek feed science/university and veterinary nutrition partnership to select a specifically permitted hemp feed ingredient and independent COA testing (cannabinoids and contaminants). No informal feed trials or rations provided here.
**Phase 5: regulated, veterinarian-designed trial only with approvals.** Obtain appropriate animal-care research and food-product protocols, milk/meat sampling/residue monitoring, veterinary nutrition formulations, withdrawal/marketing restrictions where applicable and regulator written clearance. If not approved, remain conventional-feed.
**Phase 6: commercial conditional deployment.** Only after ingredient approval, safety/residue evidence, husbandry outcomes, margin and appropriate labeling. Never advertise hemp-fed beef/milk before legal and scientific verification.

## Regular care and operations schedule (planning template; NOT an animal feed schedule)
| Cadence | Activity |
|---|---|
| Daily (once animals are acquired) | Animal welfare and appetite/body condition observations, adequate clean water and legally approved forage/feed, enclosure and shade checks, health alerts and records. Follow veterinarian/experienced stockperson for specific frequency. |
| Weekly | Check pasture condition, fencing, feed inventory, water systems, fly/parasite/hoof issues as appropriate, costs, transport and storm supplies; adjust grazing by field condition. |
| Monthly | Review animal condition, weight/production metrics, feed prices, soil/pasture records and biosecurity. Consult veterinary team where indicated. |
| Seasonal | Forage growth and drought/heat/hurricane contingencies, hay reserves, breeding/calving or milk plan, mineral program under nutritionist oversight, veterinary schedule and stocking adjustments. |
| Annual | Financial audit, animal welfare audit, soil and pasture tests, mortality/health records, feed-legality recheck and investment decision. |

## Research-to-permission milestone checklist
1. Written current species-specific legal treatment of each proposed hemp ingredient in Florida and US animal-food channels.
2. Veterinary nutrition review of actual ingredient analysis and full ration adequacy before any feed.
3. Independent cannabinoid, microbial, mycotoxin, metals and pesticide feed assays where relevant.
4. Animal welfare, biosecurity, veterinarian relationship and land carrying-capacity proof.
5. Animal-product consumer residue assessment / approvals for food-animal use.
6. Documented budget, risk reserve and separately approved acquisition/experiments.

## Regenerative integration without unsafe shortcuts
Possible legal livestock manure composting/soil nutrient recovery with pathogen controls, measured grazing effects and water management. Hemp stalks for fiber/materials and *not* presumptively for animal feed; feed applications must be eligible. Keep mushroom-composite and chemical/pyrolysis residues out of feed and pastures absent independent clearance.
