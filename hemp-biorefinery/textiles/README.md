# Community Hemp Textiles — Manufacturing & Access Program
Date: 2026-10-12. Research-stage extension of Advanced Materials & Manufacturing. No facility, product certification, funding, or purchase approved.

## Mission
Build expertise in durable hemp-based textiles for community access, practical clothing, household goods and technical fabrics, while preserving a pathway to industrial textile manufacturing. Measure affordability and utility, not only premium fashion positioning.

## Product ladder
1. **First market research:** tote bags, aprons, workwear accessories, household linens and fabric yardage using traceable third-party hemp or hemp-blend cloth; compare actual prices, durability, quality and willingness to buy.
2. **Small branded collections via manufacturing partners:** garments, uniforms, bed linens, towels, curtains and event merchandise; qualify wash shrinkage, colorfastness, abrasion and safety.
3. **Fiber supply:** cultivate/contract fiber hemp, selective harvesting, retting, decortication, scutching/hackling, degumming and cottonization with competent processing partners.
4. **Yarn and fabrics:** spun hemp and cotton/hemp blends, weaving/knitting, fabric finishing, independent material lab tests and documented fiber content.
5. **In-house textile operations:** only after contracts justify cutting/sewing, finishing, spinning/weaving, water/energy treatment and labor protections.
6. **Industrial and advanced textile expansion:** filtration, geotextiles, technical nonwovens, fiber-reinforced composite fabrics, and only qualified protective garments and aerospace textiles where tested.

## Why textile quality matters
Textile quality depends on cultivar and stalk quality, retting consistency, pectin removal/degumming, fiber length/fineness, spinning, finishing and batch variation. Basic mechanical decortication does not automatically give garment-grade yarn. Fungal/enzymatic retting may be useful but can cause strength loss if poorly controlled.

## Community benefit commitments
- Interview local buyers and nonprofits about durable essentials and affordability.
- Explore repair, reuse, cooperative contract sewing and vocational/training partnerships without assuming ownership/authorization.
- Transparent label: actual hemp fiber percentage, blended materials, origin and washing instructions; avoid unsubstantiated antimicrobial, biodegradable or climate claims.
- Keep low-cost essential textiles separate from premium margin concepts; model sustainable cross-subsidy only when economics exist.

## Research gates
G0 demand and priority items; G1 documented fiber/cloth source; G2 lab quality and finishing; G3 cut-and-sew partner and real landed price; G4 legally compliant labeling and product safety; G5 approved capped pilot and measured reorders.
No factory capital, community benefit promise or textile product preorder until readiness is proven.

## First zero-cost worklist
Compare 5 existing hemp or hemp-blend textile SKUs by product, claimed fiber content, price/unit, shipping and provenance. Identify 3 possible material suppliers and independent tests for fabric GSM, shrinkage, tear/tensile, abrasion, pilling and colorfastness. Build a one-page brief for initial community product after buyer interviews.
