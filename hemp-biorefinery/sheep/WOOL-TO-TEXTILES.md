# Sheep Wool + Hemp Textile Integration
2026-10-12 | R&D and circular supply-chain hypotheses only.

## Our manufacturing goal
We can research complementary textiles from plant fiber (hemp bast) and animal fiber (wool), plus blended hemp/wool fabrics. These are NOT interchangeable feedstock fibers: they require different cleaning, grading, spinning and performance processes.

## Wool processing chain
Humane breed-appropriate shearing -> identification and grade by flock/animal -> skirt and sort -> professional washing/scouring and effluent control -> card/comb -> spin or felt -> weave/knit/nonwoven -> finish, label, test -> finished goods.

## Hemp fiber processing chain
Legal industrial hemp cultivation/contract -> appropriate retting and decortication -> fiber cleaning and degumming/cottonization as needed -> spin/weave with qualified vendor -> test and label.

## Candidate community products
- Wool blankets and warm layers, with humidity comfort and care instructions.
- Hemp/wool work shirts or jackets, depending on yarn and manufacturing viability.
- Insulation/acoustic batts or panels, requiring independent fire, mold, moisture, fiber-dust and building-code testing.
- Yarn and repair kits, locally sourced textiles and cooperative production.
- Recycled/waste wool felt and packaging where safety and customer value support it.

## Product claims and tests
Fiber content measured and labeled according to applicable FTC textile and wool labeling requirements; wash shrinkage, warmth, durability, itch/skin comfort, abrasion, pilling, breathability, colorfastness and dimensional stability. For building panels require fire/smoke and environmental testing before claims or installation.

## Commercial gate
Actual fleece yield/animal, shearing and handling cost, cleaned yield, processing fees, labor, distribution, buyer willingness to pay and product quality must justify herd/textile investment. Wool alone is not necessarily enough to offset husbandry expenses.

## Safety separation
No chemically treated biorefinery residues in livestock feeds or bedding without specific review. Composting manure does not guarantee disease/pathogen elimination. Avoid unsupervised animal feeding experiments.
