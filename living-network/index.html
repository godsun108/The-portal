Historical context

Research timeline

Milestones are shown as parallel developments. A chronological sequence alone does not prove that one program descended from another.

1990s–2001

Berkeley Smart Dust

DARPA/MTO-supported research explored autonomous sensing and communication in very small motes. The project page records its completion in 2001 and points to follow-on areas including Berkeley Webs and NEST.

2009

Radio-controlled insect flight

Researchers demonstrated remote control of beetle flight using implanted electrodes and an electronic backpack. This is a separate biohybrid-robotics lineage, not proof of a mosquito network.

2010–2012

Engineered mosquitoes as experimental protein delivery

Laboratory studies explored transgenic mosquitoes expressing vaccine-related proteins in saliva. These were controlled experiments in mice, not evidence of public release or human genetic modification.

2020

Mosquito blood-meal DNA identification

A forensic study reported STR and SNP profiles recovered from mosquito blood meals using laboratory amplification and sequencing methods.

2023

Mixed blood-meal DNA profiling

A further study investigated STR profiling from mixed mosquito blood meals, extending the forensic evidence base.

FY2022–FY2023

Japan Moonshot biological-cyborg project

JST records JPMJMS223A as a Goal 3 project selected in FY2022 and completed in FY2023. Its stated research architecture combined biological cyborgs, miniature sensors, communications, behavior control and AI self-organization. Publications acknowledging the grant appeared later.

FY2023 close-out report

Twenty-cyborg formation and implanted electronics documented

JST’s final report describes a 10 × 10 mm electronic unit, fully implanted behavior-control hardware, and a successful 20-cockroach formation-movement experiment. The report also states that project data and source code were kept in private repositories and treated as non-public, and that the project chose not to file patents for its results.

2025

Cyborg insect swarm navigation

A Nature Communications paper reported a 20-insect swarm experiment in an obstructed environment. Its experimental positioning relied on centralized motion capture.

2025

Automated cyborg insect assembly and rescue deployment

NTU reported a robotic assembly prototype and a March 2025 humanitarian deployment of ten cyborg cockroaches in Myanmar with Singapore's Civil Defence Force. The peer-reviewed assembly paper explicitly acknowledges JST Moonshot grant JPMJMS223A, providing a direct funding link to the Morishima project.

2026

Implanted controllers and amphibious cyborg insects

NTU’s publication list records a fully implanted miniature radio controller paper that acknowledges JPMJMS223A alongside a second JST grant. A separate NTU–Waseda diving-suit project reports up to three hours of underwater activity; NTU identifies different funding and a separate patent filing, so it should not be conflated with the earlier Moonshot project’s no-patent close-out statement.

2025 onward

DARPA HyBRIDS

DARPA's program explores integration of biological and synthetic components, while acknowledging that many biohybrid platforms remain laboratory proof-of-concepts.

What moves through the system?

Sensing, transmission and control

These functions appear in published experiments, but they are not interchangeable. The Moonshot close-out report, separate NTU search-and-rescue papers, and later perception research document different sensing and data pathways.

Demonstrated · JST Moonshot close-out

Internal biological measurements

The report describes measuring neural excitation, muscle electrical activity and heartbeat, alongside body movement and environmental information. Measurements were synchronized as time-series data. It does not establish that every measurement was continuously broadcast off the insect.

Demonstrated · separate NTU research

Thermal human-presence detection

A cyborg cockroach used a low-resolution thermal array and onboard classification to detect human presence. The system sent sensor measurements and classification results wirelessly to a remote PC. This was presence detection for search and rescue, not identity recognition.

Demonstrated · JST Moonshot

Behavioral feedback and movement control

Electrical stimulation was used to influence movement. The project modeled insect behavior and designed control rules for group movement; its final report describes a 20-insect formation experiment.

In development · 2026 paper

Physiology-informed perception models

A 2026 study combines low-frequency neural features, heartbeat activity and body-motion data to estimate an insect’s internal perception under different environmental conditions. This is research on insect state and behavior, not human identity data.

Data-flow limit: the JST report says project teams exchanged data and devices and used private repositories; it does not publish a complete network diagram, radio protocol, destination list, retention policy or external access list. Those details remain unverified from the public report.
Identity limit: none of these cockroach systems is shown collecting human DNA. The separate mosquito forensic studies require laboratory extraction and genetic analysis.
Claim checking

The evidence desk

The exhibit uses consistent labels so readers can tell what has been observed, proposed or not substantiated.

Demonstrated

Insects can carry electronic control systems.

Biohybrid insect prototypes with backpacks, wireless communication and movement stimulation have been demonstrated.

Demonstrated

Human DNA can be recovered from mosquito blood meals.

Forensic laboratories have recovered human STR/SNP profiles after amplification and sequencing.

Demonstrated

Groups of cyborg insects can navigate together experimentally.

A 20-insect cockroach swarm was tested, with centralized motion-capture positioning in the reported setup.

In development

Insect-derived biosensors can convert biological recognition into electronic signals.

Research uses insect receptors and proteins in engineered sensor platforms. Field readiness varies substantially.

Proposed / incomplete

Fully decentralized, self-organizing biohybrid networks.

Research directions exist, but the degree of autonomy, sensing and networking must be checked project by project.

Not established

A mosquito-based network that identifies and tracks named people.

No reliable evidence in this archive establishes such a deployed system.

No evidence found

Mosquitoes functioning as quantum computers.

Quantum computing research and insect sensing research are separate fields; no demonstrated insect quantum computer is identified here.

No evidence found

Insects collecting DNA and transmitting a human identity profile autonomously.

DNA recovery requires laboratory processing in the documented forensic studies.

Questions still open

These are investigation prompts, not implied conclusions.

What are the exact hardware payloads, communication ranges, operating times and autonomy limits of each biohybrid insect prototype?
Which biological-sensing systems have been validated outside controlled laboratory conditions?
Are there documented funding, patent-ownership, personnel or publication links between the Berkeley, JST, NTU and DARPA programs?
