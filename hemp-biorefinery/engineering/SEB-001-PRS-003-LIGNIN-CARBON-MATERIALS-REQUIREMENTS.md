# SEB-001-PRS-003 — Lignin and Carbon Materials Product Requirements

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Draft requirements framework — R&D and customer validation required  
**Classification:** Preliminary product definition — no commercial product or graphene claim established

## 1. Purpose and scope
Define separate qualification pathways for:
1. Lignin-rich fractions intended for resin, binder, adhesive, coating or composite research.
2. Biochar intended for evaluated soil or material applications.
3. Carbon precursors and advanced-carbon materials produced through a distinct, independently validated upgrading research route.

These streams are not interchangeable. A lignin-rich fraction is not pure lignin without assay. Biochar is not graphene. Ordinary biomass pyrolysis does not establish graphene production. A graphene-like or graphitic material claim requires independent structural characterization and reproducible evidence.

## 2. Product family boundaries
| Family | Candidate form / use | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Lignin-rich fraction | Wet cake, dried powder, solution or recovered solid for resin/binder R&D | Composition and residual process chemistry must be measured |
| Lignin-derived formulation | Resin, binder, adhesive or coating research intermediate | Formulation-specific performance and customer qualification required |
| Biochar | Characterized solid for controlled soil or material trials | Must meet application-specific contaminant, stability and performance requirements |
| Carbon precursor | Qualified char or other carbon-rich feed for separate upgrading R&D | Source, composition and allocation must be traceable |
| Advanced carbon material | Graphitic/graphene-like R&D sample | Structural identity and quality must be established independently; no assumed yield or grade |

No product is approved for sale, land application, food/feed contact, environmental release or regulated use by this draft.

## 5. Advanced-carbon / graphene-like research requirements
The carbon R&D route shall be treated as a separate research program with its own feed specification, process boundary, hazards, mass balance and independent QA. It must not borrow biochar product claims or count the same carbon mass in both soil and advanced-material pathways.

Minimum evidence package:
- Traceable precursor lot and complete upstream process history.
- Precursor composition, ash/metals, moisture, particle-size distribution and carbon content.
- Documented upgrading method and process conditions under approved laboratory controls.
- Measured precursor input, product output, capture residue, off-spec material, emissions and waste.
- Independent structural characterization using appropriate complementary methods (for example, Raman spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, electron microscopy and surface/chemical analysis as appropriate to the claim).
- Explicit distinction among amorphous carbon, turbostratic/graphitic carbon, graphite and graphene-family materials; a single test or marketing label is insufficient.
- Reproducibility across independently prepared batches and documented uncertainty.
- Dust containment, exposure controls, waste handling and sample custody.

No numerical graphene yield, layer count, purity, conductivity, surface area, production cost or commercial performance is assumed. Product identity and any grade claim require independent expert review and appropriate validated methods.

## 6. Exclusive carbon and residue allocation
- Carbon allocated to soil-grade biochar is unavailable to the advanced-carbon precursor route.
- Carbon precursor allocation must be recorded as a separate measured stream.
- Pyrolysis gas, condensate, tar, ash and char are separate streams; no energy or saleable-product credit is assigned without characterization and treatment review.
- Capture residues and off-spec powders remain contained until characterized and assigned an approved recycle or disposal route.
- Carbon accounting shall distinguish feedstock biogenic carbon, product carbon, emissions, wastewater carbon and retained residues; do not claim carbon-negative or carbon-removal performance from a simple char mass.

## 7. Sampling, analytical QA and customer qualification
Establish matrix-specific sampling and preservation protocols for wet lignin liquor, lignin solids, char and fine carbon powders. Record homogenization, moisture correction, sample preparation, instrument calibration, standards, replicate count, detection limits, uncertainty and chain of custody. Independent laboratories shall be selected based on demonstrated competence for the specific analytical method.

Customer qualification shall test the material in the actual intended formulation or application. Chemical characterization alone does not demonstrate adhesive strength, composite durability, agronomic benefit, electrical performance or commercial suitability.

## 8. Packaging, storage and transport
Define packaging only after product form and hazards are known. Address moisture uptake, oxidation, dust leakage, static, incompatible materials, container compatibility, temperature, shelf-life evidence, labeling and transport requirements. Fine carbon powders require engineered containment and exposure controls; do not handle or ship them as ordinary bulk solids without review.

## 9. Safety, environmental and regulatory review
Evaluate process-specific solvent and reagent hazards, corrosive streams, hot surfaces, pyrolysis off-gas/CO, flammable vapors, pressure/vacuum systems, combustible dust, nanoparticulate/fine-powder exposure, fire protection, emissions, wastewater and contaminated residues. Determine applicable environmental and product regulations for the selected site and end use. A formal HAZID and, where warranted, process hazard analysis and industrial hygiene assessment are required before pilot operation.

## 10. Customer / research validation plan
1. Obtain customer use cases and written specifications for lignin-derived materials and biochar.
2. Identify research partners and analytical laboratories for advanced-carbon identity testing; no partnership or paid engagement is implied.
3. Establish a contained bench-scale protocol and preapproved waste/emissions controls before any physical trial.
4. Characterize all inputs, products, byproducts and losses with reconciled mass and carbon balances.
5. Repeat independent batches and have key structural claims reviewed by qualified independent specialists.
6. Advance only the pathways with credible technical performance, safe handling, regulatory viability and customer demand.

## 11. Open items and release gate
- PRS-LC-01: Select lignin application and customer specification.
- PRS-LC-02: Establish lignin assay and residual chemistry panel.
- PRS-LC-03: Define biochar end use and applicable contaminant criteria.
- PRS-LC-04: Define carbon precursor allocation and independently validated upgrading route.
- PRS-LC-05: Establish advanced-carbon identity, repeatability and containment evidence.
- PRS-LC-06: Complete application-specific regulatory and HSE review.

**No graphene/graphitic product claim, soil-use release, commercial sale or scale-up is authorized** until the relevant identity, quality, safety, regulatory, customer and independent-review gates are satisfied.
