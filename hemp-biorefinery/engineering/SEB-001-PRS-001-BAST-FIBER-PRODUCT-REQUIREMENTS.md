# SEB-001-PRS-001 — Bast Fiber Product Requirements

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Draft requirements framework — customer validation required  
**Classification:** Preliminary product definition — not a manufacturing specification or product guarantee

## 1. Purpose and scope
Define the evidence and qualification framework for bast-derived fiber products. Candidate markets include pulp and specialty paper, nonwoven and textile feedstocks, molded fiber, and natural-fiber reinforcement for polymer or mineral matrices. These are candidate applications, not approved product claims. The first commercial grade shall be selected only after customer discovery, feedstock trials, processing trials, and independent testing.

This document covers bast recovered during mechanical decortication. It does not assume that all incoming stalk mass becomes usable bast, nor that fiber from different cultivars, harvests, retting histories or storage conditions is interchangeable.

## 2. Product family and forms to evaluate
| Candidate form | Potential downstream use | Qualification questions |
|---|---|---|
| Long/technical bast fiber | Textile, yarn, specialty reinforcement | Fiber length distribution, fineness, strength, cleanliness, consistency and processability |
| Short/staple fiber | Nonwovens, molded products, pulp blends | Length distribution, fines, bonding, dispersion and contaminant profile |
| Refined pulp / bast-derived furnish | Specialty paper, packaging, molded fiber | Freeness/drainage, fiber morphology, strength, formation, brightness and chemical residues |
| Chopped or treated reinforcement | Polymer/mineral composites | Aspect ratio, surface condition, moisture, adhesion, dispersion and composite performance |

Do not claim textile-grade, food-contact, medical, automotive, construction or other regulated suitability without application-specific customer qualification and regulatory review.

## 3. Feedstock and traceability requirements
Each production lot shall be traceable to grower/source, cultivar where known, field/region, harvest date, retting or field-conditioning history, storage conditions, receipt date, and incoming lot ID. Record wet receipt mass, moisture method and result, dry-basis mass, visible contamination, odor/mold observations, and segregation status.

Decortication trials shall separately weigh bast, hurd, fines/dust and unaccounted losses. Report each fraction on both as-received and dry basis. Record machine configuration, throughput, power, screen settings, fiber damage and operating interruptions. No assumed bast recovery percentage is permitted.

## 5. Sampling, preparation and quality control
- Establish a written sampling plan by product form and lot size; collect spatially distributed increments and retain a sealed archive sample.
- Record sample conditioning, humidity/temperature, preparation equipment, operator, date, and chain of custody.
- Use representative subsamples for length, fineness, strength, moisture, ash and contamination testing.
- Report replicate count, raw results, mean, spread, measurement uncertainty and any excluded observations.
- Define control samples, calibration checks, retest rules, nonconformance handling and lot release authority before routine production.
- Do not blend nonconforming lots to conceal variability; any blending requires traceable mass accounting and customer approval.

## 6. Product processing and manufacturing interfaces
Potential operations include opening, cleaning, screening, cutting, carding, refining, pulping, washing, drying, surface treatment and packaging. Selection depends on the intended product form. Equipment duties, process aids, water use, energy use, wastewater, dust collection and fiber loss remain TBD pending trials and vendor data.

Bast allocation is exclusive: fiber sold or used as reinforcement cannot simultaneously be assigned to pulp or chemical conversion in the mass balance. Any rejects or fines transferred to another pathway require a measured transfer and updated stream accounting.

## 7. Packaging, storage and transport
Packaging format, bale density, moisture protection, palletization, labeling, maximum storage duration and transport conditions shall be defined with customers. The control plan shall address moisture ingress, biological degradation, contamination, fiber entanglement, dust release, fire load and safe handling. Do not assign a shelf life without stability evidence.

## 8. Safety, regulatory and documentation
Assess combustible dust and fiber handling hazards, machinery guarding, noise, manual handling, fire protection, housekeeping, and worker exposure. Evaluate chemical residues and restrictions based on the actual process and intended market. Maintain lot certificate / test report, safety information where applicable, origin and traceability records, change notices, and nonconformance records.

No claim of compliance with a product, building, textile, packaging, food-contact or environmental standard is made by this draft. The applicable requirements must be identified for each intended customer and jurisdiction.

## 9. Customer validation plan
1. Interview prospective customers and obtain current written specifications and sample acceptance protocols.
2. Select no more than a small number of candidate grades based on demonstrated demand and available feedstock.
3. Produce controlled bench/pilot samples from traceable lots; record complete process and mass data.
4. Submit samples only after owner approval and appropriate safety / shipping review.
5. Obtain written test feedback, processing observations, qualification gaps and commercial volume/consistency expectations.
6. Revise the grade definition and release criteria only through document control.

## 10. Open items and release gate
- PRS-BF-01: Choose priority application and customer segment.
- PRS-BF-02: Obtain customer specifications and accepted test methods.
- PRS-BF-03: Characterize bast yield, fiber damage and lot variability.
- PRS-BF-04: Establish product form, processing route and packaging.
- PRS-BF-05: Complete HSE, regulatory and customer qualification review.

**Product release is not authorized** until the specification contains measurable acceptance limits, validated methods, an approved sampling plan, demonstrated process capability, and designated QA release authority.
