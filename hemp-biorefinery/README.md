# Industrial Hemp Biorefinery — Research & Systems Design
Established 2026-10-12. **Feasibility stage. No equipment ordered, operating plant, engineering certification, permits, product sales or capital authorization.**

## Flagship: advanced materials for the world we build
**The primary long-term industrial mission is to develop and qualify regenerative materials for skyscrapers, boats, trains, automobiles, aircraft, spaceships, computers, robotics, and equipment used to manufacture future materials.** The hemp/mycelium biorefinery is the feedstock and processing platform; materials engineering and application-specific qualification are the ultimate manufacturing capabilities.

## Mission
Design an integrated, staged processing facility that separates industrial hemp stalks into bast fiber, hurd, and eventually purified cellulose, hemicellulose-derived C5 sugars (especially xylose), lignin and conversion coproducts. Evaluate profitable product paths for **paper, biobased plastics and composites, industrial sugars, and gasoline/diesel-range renewable hydrocarbon fuel blendstocks**. Invest only after mass balance, safety/permitting and actual unit economics justify the sequence.

## Priority clarification
The term "cross-current reduction machine" is not a verified standard one-machine designation. Investigate **cross-current and countercurrent extraction, hydrothermal/dilute-acid pretreatment, organosolv, alkaline processing, enzymatic hydrolysis and solvent recovery**, and compare real vendor equipment. Countercurrent usually refers to movement of extracting liquid opposite to solids, while cross-current stages use fresh extraction liquid in successive contacts. Neither is a one-step guarantee of pure sugar/cellulose/lignin.

## Primary material architecture
1. Hemp stalk supply: cultivar/legal compliance, acreage/yield, moisture, storage, transport and seasonal supply.
2. Screening/drying/chopping and mechanical decortication: separate valuable **bast** from woody **hurd** and fines.
3. Bast branch: preserve long fibers for **papermaking/pulp** or biocomposite reinforcement. Alternate route: dissolve/hydrolyze fibers for glucose; don't double-count.
4. Hurd branch: pretreatment/fractionation and controlled separation.
5. **Hemicellulose fraction** -> C5-rich liquor -> hydrolysis, conditioning, filtration -> purified xylose or fermentation/chemical intermediates. A liquor is **not** automatically food-grade sugar; purification and legal food intended use is a distinct expensive project.
6. **Cellulose-rich fraction** -> paper pulp / molded fiber / bioplastics reinforcement **OR** enzymatic hydrolysis -> glucose -> biochemicals/fuels. Saleable cellulose/paper and glucose fuels compete for the same cellulose mass.
7. **Lignin-rich fraction** -> qualified lignin resin/binder/carbon precursor **OR** energy/thermochemical products; chemistry and contamination determine suitability.
8. Residuals and utilities -> wastewater treatment, heat integration, reagent recovery, emissions control, biomass waste, energy and water balancing.

## Product-lane decision hierarchy
- **Nearer-term demonstration**: mechanical separation, graded hurd/bast product specs, paper/pulp/composite customers.
- **Mid-term lab/pilot**: xylose-rich hydrolysate, defined sugar purification, lignin separation and polymer filler or resin trials.
- **Long-term industrial integration**: fuel-grade hydrocarbons via biochemical/catalytic refining, engineered fermentation products/bioplastics and full process heat/recovery. Gasoline and diesel blends require product standards, licensing, fuel testing and substantial industrial capital.
This is a *research-order hypothesis*, not a claim about actual profitability.

## Six gates
**G0 evidence:** hemp feedstock availability, custody, independently tested composition.
**G1 process:** certified engineer's process block flow, yields, recovery, mass/energy/water balances.
**G2 product:** exact offtake specs for pulp, sugars, lignin, resin/plastics and fuels.
**G3 compliance:** worker safety, air/wastewater/waste permits, fuel/chemical regulations, equipment pressure/fire/explosion protections, local land use and hemp requirements.
**G4 economics:** comparable vendor process quotes, delivered ton cost, energy/water/reagent costs, labor/maintenance and independently reviewed TEA/LCA.
**G5 authorization:** budget, capped loss, responsible entity, qualified engineers, stage-specific milestones and no purchase/contract unless approved.

## Full-circle regenerative campus extension (October 12, 2026)
We are expanding the **research scope**, not claiming an operating circular refinery. Eligible clean residual biomass may be allocated to engineered pyrolysis and tested soil-grade biochar, or carbon-material conversion research. A dedicated experimental route examines whether hemp-derived biochar can be upgraded to characterized graphene-like carbon for material composites. Segregate soil amendment biochar from high-performance carbon fillers and enforce measured mass/energy, emissions, wastewater and carbon accounting. All equipment and operations remain subject to qualified engineering, legal review and future budget authorization.
