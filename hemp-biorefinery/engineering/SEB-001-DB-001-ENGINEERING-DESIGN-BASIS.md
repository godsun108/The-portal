# SEB-001-DB-001 — Engineering Design Basis

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Draft for engineering review  
**Classification:** Preliminary Engineering Basis — Not for Construction

## 1. Project definition
SEB-001 is a modular Florida industrial hemp biorefinery and advanced-materials development campus. The plant is intended to receive legally sourced industrial hemp stalks, mechanically separate bast and hurd, fractionate suitable hurd through a continuous multistage cross-current process, recover useful cellulose-, hemicellulose-/C5-sugar-, and lignin-rich streams, and evaluate downstream manufacturing and thermal-carbon pathways. The flagship strategy is to develop the product families together, with staged investment and expansion governed by independently verified product quality, market demand, safety, permitting, and profitability.

## 3. Capacity and operating basis
- Initial nameplate feed: **1,000 kg/day dry whole hemp stalk**.
- Initial operating schedule: **16 h/day**, **250 operating days/year**.
- Initial dry-feed rate: 1,000 / 16 = **62.5 kg/h**.
- Initial annual dry feed: 1,000 × 250 = **250,000 kg/y = 250 dry t/y**.
- Future capacity modules: 5, 10 and 25 dry t/day. At 250 operating days/y these correspond to 1,250, 2,500 and 6,250 dry t/y respectively.
- Layout and utility corridors should consider future 24 h/day operation, but no 24-hour production rate or annual tonnage is assumed in the initial economic case.
- “Dry tonne” means metric tonne of feedstock solids on a measured oven-dry or validated moisture-corrected basis. Wet receipt mass must be measured separately.

## 4. Site and layout basis
Planning envelope: **5–10 acres** on a separate Florida industrial parcel near Sacred Earth Center 001. This is a planning assumption, not a parcel selection or land-use determination. Reserve segregated zones for receiving/storage; mechanical preparation/decortication; wet fractionation and chemical containment; wastewater and solvent/reagent recovery; thermal conversion; carbon/graphene R&D; product fabrication; utilities; laboratory/QA; finished goods; fire access and future modules. Establish prevailing wind, flood elevation, drainage, setbacks, road access, fire water, utility service and environmental constraints only after parcel-specific studies.

## 5. Feedstock basis
Feedstock is legally compliant industrial hemp stalk, with traceable grower/source records. Whole-stalk moisture, ash, grit, metals, cultivar, harvest condition, storage degradation, bast/hurd split, particle-size distribution and chemical composition are unknown at this revision. Do not apply hurd literature composition to whole stalk without measured decortication split and representative assays. Sampling and analysis shall be performed by qualified laboratories using documented methods.

## 6. Process basis and boundaries
1. Receive, weigh, sample, inspect and segregate feedstock.
2. Dry/condition and size-reduce as justified by vendor testing.
3. Decorticate and screen into bast, hurd and fines; record all fractions.
4. Preserve bast for fiber/pulp/reinforcement products or explicitly allocate to an alternate route; never count the same fiber mass twice.
5. Route suitable hurd to continuous multistage cross-current fractionation pilot/vendor-selected process.
6. Recover cellulose-rich solids, hemicellulose-derived liquor, lignin-rich stream, wash streams and residuals; measure losses and added chemicals.
7. Evaluate purification, pulp/composite, xylose, lignin/resin, and other downstream product routes against customer specifications.
8. Route only eligible, segregated biomass residuals to controlled thermal conversion. Soil-grade biochar and carbon-material feed are mutually exclusive allocations of the same char mass.
9. Graphene-like carbon upgrading is a separate R&D unit operation, not an assumed direct output of ordinary pyrolysis.

## 7. Design philosophy
- Modular, maintainable, expandable and instrumented.
- Vendor-neutral until process route and feed data are verified.
- Segregate wet chemistry, hot thermal conversion, food/biological uses, dust-producing mechanical handling, and nanoscale carbon work.
- Prefer closed-loop reagent/solvent recovery where technically and environmentally justified; do not assume 100% recovery.
- Establish full mass, water, energy, carbon, waste and emissions balances before commercial design.
- Use purchased, certified safety-critical machinery and controls; in-house fabrication is limited to noncritical components after qualification.

## 8. Design conditions and standards
Final design conditions, pressure/temperature ratings, materials of construction, electrical classification, fire protection, structural loads, wind/flood criteria, and applicable Florida/local codes remain parcel- and process-specific. Qualified licensed professionals shall identify the governing editions of applicable building, fire, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, pressure equipment, environmental, occupational safety, and hazardous-area standards. No specific code compliance is claimed by this preliminary basis.

## 9. Acceptance criteria for the next design gate
- Representative feedstock data with moisture and composition uncertainty reported.
- Process alternatives compared on common feed basis, with vendor/lab evidence.
- Closed and reconciled mass balance with explicit reaction water, reagent additions, gas, wastewater and losses.
- Preliminary PFDs with stream IDs and battery limits.
- Utility loads supported by vendor data or explicitly ranged assumptions.
- HAZID / process hazard review and environmental permitting register.
- Product specifications and at least preliminary customer validation.
- Cost estimate basis, estimate class, uncertainty range, exclusions and contingencies.
- Owner approval of the next stage budget before paid work or procurement.
