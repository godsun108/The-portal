# PORTAL Reality Bridge

A game may deliberately blur the boundary between its fictional world and ordinary life.

Examples: discover a signal by visiting a broad public region; unlock lore after walking; scan an AR artifact; complete a daylight/night mission; find a type of public place; let weather or local events alter the fictional world.

Design rules:
- Use coarse proofs where possible, not a permanent trail of raw coordinates.
- Never require dangerous, restricted, private, roadway, wilderness, or trespass locations.
- Every reality-gated critical-path objective gets an accessible in-game alternate.
- Real-world tasks should encourage exploration, movement, observation or collaboration—not spending money.
- Do not expose one player's precise location to other players.
- Minors and privacy-sensitive users need reality features disabled or strongly constrained by default.
- The fiction must not misrepresent real emergencies, authorities, or dangerous instructions as gameplay.

Architecture: Reality Bridge emits verified gameplay claims (region/category/activity/time/AR) into the campaign/world runtime. The game consumes the claim, not an unnecessary location history.

Potential modes: Reality Campaign, optional Field Missions, city/community events, shared AR encounters, scavenger hunts, environmental observation, fitness/adventure quests, and cross-device TV + phone play.
