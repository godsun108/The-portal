# MycoCircular — Hemp + Mushroom Manufacturing Research
Established 2026-10-12. Research and systems design only. No facility, yields, profits, safety certifications, capital or contracts are established.

## Mission
Integrate a modular fungal biotechnology and mushroom-manufacturing operation with the regenerative hemp biorefinery. Use verified clean hemp hurd and other qualifying substrates for selected edible mushrooms, biologically grown packaging, panels and engineered composite materials, and evaluate enzyme pathways for biomass processing. Route *eligible* spent substrate through measured recovery pathways. Build toward making **some of our own factory components** using hemp-fiber/char/mycelium composites while externally sourcing certified safety-critical machinery.

## Architecture
Field / hemp stalks -> processing and decortication -> segregated, traceable hemp hurd and bast.
- Product lane 1: food-grade qualified cultivation substrate -> edible mushroom fruiting -> refrigerated produce sales or legally compliant processed food. Spent substrate is separate by source and composition.
- Product lane 2: qualified biomass -> controlled mycelium composite growing/molding -> inactivation/drying/QA -> protective packing inserts, acoustic products or nonstructural panels.
- Product lane 3: dedicated fungal enzymes / biomass pretreatment R&D -> test whether delignification helps sugar recovery without excessive consumption of C5/C6 carbohydrates.
- Materials lane: hemp bast + suitable matrices / carbon fillers -> molded housings, fixtures, pallets, partitions and engineered noncritical components, after mechanical/fire/durability qualification.
- Remainder: spent mushroom substrate -> select *one* traceable allocation per mass batch: independently qualified compost/soil, biochar, anaerobic digestion, or subsequent chemical/material processing. Don't double count.
- Utilities: heat and water recovery where feasible, controlled humidity/ventilation, segregated drainage, waste management and metered energy.

## Critical zones
1. Raw hemp storage and dry biomass sorting, traceability.
2. Food mushroom substrate prep and hygienic production (isolated from industrial chemicals, dust, pyrolysis and raw compost).
3. Food fruiting/harvest/cooling/packing with food-quality controls and trained staff.
4. Separate composite fabrication / molds / drying / testing, with occupational spore/dust and moisture controls.
5. Separate enzyme research and biomass analysis lab; no transfer to food production without appropriate approval.
6. Spent substrate recovery / contained compost or offsite permitted pyrolysis/biodigestion.
7. Fabrication/tool room and lab for carbon/polymer/fiber engineering, isolated from food.
8. Mass, quality, emissions and revenue ledger.

## Science and business realism
- Oyster and lion's mane mushrooms are familiar specialty markets; each strain/substrate needs food-safe qualification and local customer evidence.
- Hemp hurd may be a substrate or blend component, not guaranteed to grow any fungus well. Test clean substrate compatibility, contamination, yields and costs in approved trials.
- Mushroom production and mycelium composite production have competing substrate demands and distinct environmental/QA requirements.
- Some mycelium products are protective packaging; structural machinery and industrial pressure/rotating components require certified materials and independent engineering.
- Nutraceutical/medicinal extracts are a different regulated and claims-sensitive venture; do not assume therapeutic benefit, FDA approval or food-use eligibility from tradition.
- Fungal delignification may lower harsh chemical use in selected processes, but incubation time, yield loss and contamination can make it uneconomic.
- "Zero waste", "carbon negative" and "maximum profit" remain testable hypotheses, not operational facts.
