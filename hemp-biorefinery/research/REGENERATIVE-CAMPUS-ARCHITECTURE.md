# Regenerative Hemp Biorefinery — Integrated Campus Architecture
Date: 2026-10-12 | Concept only; no engineering design, permits, investment, startup or performance claims.

## Vision
A modular industrial hemp facility recovering fiber, xylose/glucose streams, lignin, certified-quality biochar, and prospectively biomass-derived graphene-like carbon. Product units may supply hemp-reinforced polymers, fiberboard/paper, composites and (later) fuel intermediates. Carbon accounting and soil outcomes must be measured—not presumed.

## Top-level flow (all arrows represent potential *allocation*, not additional free material)
Hemp field -> audited farming practices / tested stalk supply -> bale storage -> size reduction / decortication
  - Bast -> paper, woven/nonwoven or engineered reinforcement.
  - Hurd -> selectively allocated:
    - fractionation -> C5 sugar liquor / cellulose solids / isolated lignin -> paper, chemicals, composites or future fuel conversion.
    - qualified dry biomass -> controlled pyrolysis -> tested biochar + condensable oils / gases / process heat (with emissions controls).
  - Clean residues/offcuts, if chemically uncontaminated and tested -> optional pyrolysis; solvent-laden, halogenated, chemically treated or otherwise unsuitable waste must NOT enter soil biochar.
  - Eligible lignin -> resin/binder/functional carbon material studies, or energy depending on analysis.
Biochar allocation (separate, non-double-counted branches):
  - soil-grade, tested biochar -> agronomic trials, compost blending, measured soil restoration.
  - technically appropriate carbon feed -> R&D upgrading -> graphene-like/graphitic carbon (not soil carbon removal).
  - possible activated carbon for permitted filtration products (separate activation/qualification).
Pyrolysis vapors / recoverable process heat -> properly engineered combustion/heat recovery or advanced valorization (with air emission controls). Do not assume pyrolysis gas can be burned or used directly in engines without treatment.
Water -> cascading process-water reuse if chemistry allows; effluent -> licensed treatment and testing.
Nutrients -> test ash/char/compost suitability; do NOT assume every ash or effluent stream belongs back on farms.
Energy -> heat integration, electricity sourcing options, measured energy input and output.
Products -> documented end use, life cycle, product durability and safe disposal/recycling where feasible.

## Proposed physical zones
1. Feedstock gate: legal hemp origin, grower contracts, dry storage, test/traceability.
2. Decortication and mechanical fiber preparation; explosion/dust protection.
3. Wet biorefinery/sugar separation; dedicated chemical containment, solvent recovery and wastewater.
4. Thermal conversion zone: appropriately designed pyrolysis, flue gas treatment, hot solids and fire protection.
5. Carbon finishing and graphene R&D: separate high-current electrical installation, dust containment, material characterization and occupational exposure control.
6. Material fabrication: fiber compounding, sheet/board/paper trials, molded packaging and customer spec testing.
7. Field validation: isolated soil test plots using certified/tested biochar; control plots, baseline measurement, drainage monitoring.
8. Independent metrology: incoming and outgoing mass, moisture, elemental carbon, heavy metals/PAHs, energy, water and emissions.

## Material allocation rules
- A tonne of hurd used to make xylose is not another tonne available for pyrolysis; only verified residual streams may be carried forward.
- Biochar sold for soil application is not also sold as graphene feedstock.
- Pyrolysis and graphene upgrading consume energy and may release carbon; graphene output is NOT automatically a durable carbon removal credit.
- Hemp fiber / plastic / paper require different quality criteria; not all products can derive from the same grade of feedstock.
- Moisture, reagent additions, gas production and ash must be tracked with consistent boundaries to avoid fraudulent mass balances.

## Product-family hypotheses
A. Nearer term: marketable bast paper/fiber, biochar meeting soil-product standards, hemp fiber polymer reinforcement from externally sourced permitted resins.
B. Development: cellulose-based molded packaging, hurd panels and biochar-filled construction products, lignin binder trials.
C. Experimental: hemp-biochar-derived turbostratic graphene or graphene-like carbon as reinforcement/conductive additive in polymer composites, concrete, coatings and carbon-based components.
D. Long term: purified xylose / derivatives, C6 conversion, renewable hydrocarbon intermediates, higher-purity lignin chemicals and advanced engineered graphene materials.

## Regeneration criteria
Environmental audit must measure: soil organic carbon and agronomic performance relative to a control; responsible harvest/residue retention; nutrient budgets, erosion/soil compaction, freshwater consumption, process emissions, product lifecycle and waste; potential biodiversity effects.
“No waste,” “net-zero,” “carbon negative,” “fully biodegradable” and “graphene” claims require independent analysis and product-specific tests.

## Non-negotiable permitting
Land use and fire codes; air emissions from drying, pyrolysis and combustion; industrial wastewater; waste streams; dust / combustible dust; occupational nanoscale particle exposure; electrical/pressure safety; chemical storage and transport; hemp feedstock chain of custody.
Qualified engineers and local regulators must determine the actual requirements.
