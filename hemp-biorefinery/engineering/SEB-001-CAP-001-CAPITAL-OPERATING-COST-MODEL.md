# SEB-001-CAP-001 — Capital and Operating Cost Model

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Cost framework only; no estimate authorized

## 1. Purpose and rule
Provide a transparent structure for developing a staged capital and operating cost estimate. No unsupported dollar figure, revenue, payback, IRR, margin or financing assumption is asserted. Existing project records explicitly require evidence before assigning $/ton, yields or fuel costs.

## 2. Estimate basis
Initial feed basis: 1 dry t/day, 16 h/day, 250 operating days/year = 250 dry t/year. Expansion modules: 5, 10 and 25 dry t/day. Site concept: separate 5–10-acre Florida industrial parcel near Sacred Earth Center 001. Estimate currency: USD. Price base date, escalation, exchange rates, freight origin, tax and duty treatment are TBD.

## 3. Capital cost breakdown structure
1. Land, due diligence, survey, geotechnical, environmental assessments and legal.
2. Site preparation, grading, stormwater, roads, fencing, drainage and hurricane/flood resilience.
3. Buildings, foundations, process structures, warehouses and laboratories.
4. Feedstock receiving, storage, conditioning, size reduction and decortication.
5. Fractionation, separation, washing, reagent/solvent storage and recovery.
6. C5, cellulose/pulp and lignin product conditioning.
7. Wastewater treatment, air emissions control and waste handling.
8. Thermal conversion, off-gas treatment, char cooling and storage.
9. Graphene/carbon R&D enclosure, electrical systems, capture and analytics.
10. Product fabrication, packaging and warehouse equipment.
11. Electrical, controls, instrumentation, fire protection, utilities and IT/OT.
12. Engineering, project management, construction management, commissioning, training and owner costs.
13. Contingency, escalation, permitting and financing costs, each shown separately.

## 4. Operating cost breakdown
Feedstock purchase/growing and inbound logistics; labor and benefits; reagents/solvents/enzymes and makeup; electricity, thermal energy, water and wastewater; maintenance/spares; lab and QA; waste disposal; packaging; outbound freight; insurance; property costs; regulatory compliance; and corporate overhead. Record unit basis, vendor/source, date, escalation and uncertainty for every cost.

## 5. Model equations
Annual feed = nameplate dry t/day × operating days/year × achieved utilization.
Annual product quantity = annual dry feed × measured product yield (dry product basis) × saleable quality fraction.
Annual net sales = sum(product quantity × net realized selling price) less rebates, commissions and freight as defined.
Annual cash operating cost = sum(feedstock, chemicals, utilities, labor, maintenance, QA, waste, logistics and overhead).
Working capital = inventory + receivables − payables, using documented days assumptions.
Project cash flow shall separately show capital expenditure, startup/ramp-up, tax, debt service, replacement capital and terminal assumptions. Do not conflate EBITDA, operating cash flow and free cash flow.

## 6. Estimate maturity and uncertainty
At this revision, the model is a Class 5 / order-of-magnitude framework only; it is not a priced estimate. No accuracy range is claimed until a recognized estimate classification is selected by the project controls lead and supporting vendor data exists. Report base, low and high cases with quantified source uncertainty; separate quoted equipment, installation factors, owner costs, contingency and escalation. Do not apply a single blanket multiplier without explaining the basis.

## 7. Scenario structure
- Stage 1: 1 dry t/day demonstration / initial module.
- Stage 2: 5 dry t/day.
- Stage 3: 10 dry t/day.
- Stage 4: 25 dry t/day.
Each stage requires independent process, product, permitting, safety and economic gates. Graphene R&D should be separately costed and excluded from base-case revenue until a verified material and buyer exist.

## 8. Acceptance criteria
Every cost has a source, date, scope, currency, quantity basis and confidence. Capacity and product allocation reconcile to MB-001. Capex includes installation and owner costs, with contingency separate. Opex uses measured/vendor utility and reagent rates. Revenue requires validated product specifications and credible offtake evidence. Sensitivities include feedstock cost/availability, yield, uptime, energy, water, product price, waste cost, capex and financing. Independent TEA review before investment decisions.
