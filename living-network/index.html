Historical context

Research timeline

Milestones are shown as parallel developments. A chronological sequence alone does not prove that one program descended from another.

1990s–2001

Berkeley Smart Dust

DARPA/MTO-supported research explored autonomous sensing and communication in very small motes. The project page records its completion in 2001 and points to follow-on areas including Berkeley Webs and NEST.

2009

Radio-controlled insect flight

Researchers demonstrated remote control of beetle flight using implanted electrodes and an electronic backpack. This is a separate biohybrid-robotics lineage, not proof of a mosquito network.

2010–2012

Engineered mosquitoes as experimental protein delivery

Laboratory studies explored transgenic mosquitoes expressing vaccine-related proteins in saliva. These were controlled experiments in mice, not evidence of public release or human genetic modification.

2020

Mosquito blood-meal DNA identification

A forensic study reported STR and SNP profiles recovered from mosquito blood meals using laboratory amplification and sequencing methods.

2023

Mixed blood-meal DNA profiling

A further study investigated STR profiling from mixed mosquito blood meals, extending the forensic evidence base.

FY2022–FY2023

Japan Moonshot biological-cyborg project

JST records JPMJMS223A as a Goal 3 project selected in FY2022 and completed in FY2023. Its stated research architecture combined biological cyborgs, miniature sensors, communications, behavior control and AI self-organization. Publications acknowledging the grant appeared later.

FY2023 close-out report

Twenty-cyborg formation and implanted electronics documented

JST’s final report describes a 10 × 10 mm electronic unit, fully implanted behavior-control hardware, and a successful 20-cockroach formation-movement experiment. The report also states that project data and source code were kept in private repositories and treated as non-public, and that the project chose not to file patents for its results.

2025

Cyborg insect swarm navigation

A Nature Communications paper reported a 20-insect swarm experiment in an obstructed environment. Its experimental positioning relied on centralized motion capture.

2025

Automated cyborg insect assembly and rescue deployment

NTU reported a robotic assembly prototype and a March 2025 humanitarian deployment of ten cyborg cockroaches in Myanmar with Singapore's Civil Defence Force. The peer-reviewed assembly paper explicitly acknowledges JST Moonshot grant JPMJMS223A, providing a direct funding link to the Morishima project.

2026

Implanted controllers and amphibious cyborg insects

NTU’s publication list records a fully implanted miniature radio controller paper that acknowledges JPMJMS223A alongside a second JST grant. A separate NTU–Waseda diving-suit project reports up to three hours of underwater activity; NTU identifies different funding and a separate patent filing, so it should not be conflated with the earlier Moonshot project’s no-patent close-out statement.

2025 onward

DARPA HyBRIDS

DARPA's program explores integration of biological and synthetic components, while acknowledging that many biohybrid platforms remain laboratory proof-of-concepts.

What moves through the system?

Sensing, transmission and control

These functions appear in published experiments, but they are not interchangeable. The Moonshot close-out report, separate NTU search-and-rescue papers, and later perception research document different sensing and data pathways.

Demonstrated · JST Moonshot close-out

Internal biological measurements

The report describes measuring neural excitation, muscle electrical activity and heartbeat, alongside body movement and environmental information. Measurements were synchronized as time-series data. It does not establish that every measurement was continuously broadcast off the insect.

Demonstrated · separate NTU research

Thermal human-presence detection

A cyborg cockroach used a low-resolution thermal array and onboard classification to detect human presence. The system sent sensor measurements and classification results wirelessly to a remote PC. This was presence detection for search and rescue, not identity recognition.

Demonstrated · JST Moonshot

Behavioral feedback and movement control

Electrical stimulation was used to influence movement. The project modeled insect behavior and designed control rules for group movement; its final report describes a 20-insect formation experiment.

In development · 2026 paper

Physiology-informed perception models

A 2026 study combines low-frequency neural features, heartbeat activity and body-motion data to estimate an insect’s internal perception under different environmental conditions. This is research on insect state and behavior, not human identity data.

Published communications topologies

2023 · NTU search-and-rescue prototype

Insect backpack ⇄ central station ⇄ PC

The backpack used Bluetooth 5.1 at 2.4 GHz. A central station connected it to a PC. In one motion-capture navigation mode, Vicon coordinates passed through the PC and central station to the insect at 30 ms intervals; onboard stimulation commands were also relayed back to the PC for logging. Thermal images were stored in onboard flash and extracted later, while human-presence results were wirelessly sent to the PC. This is a hub-and-spoke research setup, not an insect-to-insect mesh.

2023 paper ↗

2024 · Osaka University prototype

Insect backpack ⇄ wireless receiver ⇄ PC interface

This separate JST-funded study used an nRF24L01 radio module. The backpack performed obstacle feedback and thermal human-presence classification onboard; IMU/ToF measurements and derived thermal features or recognition results were sent to a PC through a wireless receiver. The reported backpack battery supported about 25 minutes of operation. The nRF24L01 is a 2.4 GHz transceiver. The paper describes a PC interface and remote operator commands, not a peer-to-peer insect mesh.

2024 paper ↗

2025 · 20-insect swarm experiment

Local-neighbor algorithm + centralized experimental localization

The swarm paper proposes a decentralized control algorithm in which insects use nearby-neighbor information, with one leader given the goal. But the authors explicitly state that the experiment supplied position data from a centralized VICON motion-capture system. They identify direct decentralized localization as future work using IMUs plus UWB ranging. A Sub-1 GHz antenna appears on the backpack; that alone does not establish that insects exchanged messages directly with one another.

2025 swarm paper ↗

2026 · implanted controller

Remote control link; telemetry not established

The 10 × 10 × 3 mm implanted controller uses sub-1 GHz communication and electrical output to stimulate the insect for maneuvering. The study reports a 26 m communication distance to its central board and 32.05 ± 1.00 minutes of actual operation (four trials). The paper establishes remote stimulation/control. It does not establish a return telemetry stream, continuous sensor upload, peer networking or a global receiver infrastructure.

2026 paper ↗

What the topology shows: the strongest documented pattern is an insect-mounted device communicating with a nearby receiver, central station or PC. The swarm-control paper is an important advance, but its own authors say decentralized sensing was not yet achieved in the experiment. No reviewed source specifies a global routing layer, internet backhaul, universal command center, cross-project shared network, or mosquito-based telemetry.
Data-flow limit: the JST report says project teams exchanged data and devices and used private repositories; it does not publish a complete network diagram, radio protocol, destination list, retention policy or external access list. Those details remain unverified from the public report.
Data access and security disclosures: the reviewed papers do not provide a complete security or governance specification for the radio links—such as encryption, device authentication, key management, radio range for the older prototypes, or a production access-control model. This means those details are undisclosed in these publications, not that the systems are necessarily insecure. The 2025 assembly paper makes its supporting data and code available in the article and supplements; the 2026 implanted-controller paper says supporting data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. The JST Moonshot close-out report separately describes private task repositories and non-public experimental data due to confidentiality.
Identity limit: none of these cockroach systems is shown collecting human DNA. The separate mosquito forensic studies require laboratory extraction and genetic analysis.

Technical claims, intellectual property & governance

Patents, autonomy & oversight

The newest research sharpens the picture: internal biological signals can now be used in a laboratory feedback controller, and patent filings cover practical insect-electrode hardware. Neither fact demonstrates a global insect network or autonomous peer-to-peer communications.

Published laboratory study · 2026

Insect Synergy Circuit (ISC)

A May 2026 paper from Keisuke Morishima's Osaka University team combines low-frequency neural amplitude, heartbeat and IMU motion data. A Bluetooth 5.0 backpack transmits measurements for machine-learning classification; stimulation decisions then bias the cockroach's movement. The proposed broader ecosystem vision is explicitly a concept, not a demonstrated multi-species communications network.

Measured limitation: online classification/control accuracy was 65.22%, versus 93.0% offline. The closed-loop evaluation used four insects in a structured multi-chamber maze. Motion capture was used for post-experiment trajectory analysis, not the real-time control loop.

Architecture distinction

What would be needed for a unified network?

At minimum, the system would need compatible sensing interfaces, power, localization, communications protocols, routing, identity and access controls, data storage, cybersecurity, maintenance and legal governance. Evidence for one component cannot be used as proof that the complete stack exists.

Key finding: Smart Dust is principally a miniature electronic-node problem; unattended ground sensors are a low-power event-detection problem; cyborg insects are mobile biohybrid-robotics platforms; microbial biosensors are biological recognition systems. The technologies may be composable in principle, but the reviewed sources do not establish a fielded system combining all four.
Data flows, control points & safeguards

Who controls the information?

A sensor does not need to identify a person to create sensitive information. Risk changes as observations are combined, retained, linked to identities or used to trigger decisions. This section maps the data lifecycle and the control points that determine who can see, reuse or act on it.

Proposed architecture · 2026

1. Collection: the observation layer

One Health sensing proposals span physiological, animal, plant and environmental observations. A reading may be a temperature, pollutant level, movement trace, acoustic event, pathogen marker or molecular measurement. The Nature Sensors perspective describes linking these domains, but it is a proposed architecture rather than proof of a deployed global system.

Read the One Health perspective ↗

Architecture option

2. Edge processing: decide what leaves the sensor

Local processing can convert raw measurements into limited event summaries and reduce unnecessary transmission. It does not automatically guarantee privacy: a derived feature or location/time pattern can still be identifying, and model outputs can reveal sensitive inferences. A defensible design specifies which raw data are kept, which features are transmitted, and how long each is retained.

Architecture option

3. Transport: authenticate devices and protect data

Network controls should verify device identity, restrict interfaces, protect data in transit and at rest, authorize configuration changes and updates, and expose security-state information to authorized operators. NIST's IoT baseline names these as core device cybersecurity capabilities. They are engineering controls, not a substitute for lawful purpose or privacy governance.

NIST IoT capability catalog ↗

Architecture option

4. Aggregation: the re-identification boundary

Combining datasets can make individually ordinary observations more revealing. Persistent location traces, timestamps, movement patterns, physiological features and external records may jointly support inference or identity linkage. Data minimization, separation of identity keys, purpose limitation, retention limits and access logging should be designed before datasets are joined—not added after a breach or misuse.

Governance requirement

5. Analysis: AI inference is not ground truth

Models can produce false positives, miss events, drift across environments or infer more than a sensor directly measured. Systems should preserve provenance, uncertainty, model version, validation conditions and human review for consequential decisions. A prediction about a person, animal or location must not be presented as a direct observation.

Governance requirement

6. Action: separate detection from authority

An alert should not itself authorize intervention. Define who may respond, what evidence is required, what proportionality rules apply, how affected parties can challenge errors, and how actions are audited. Emergency or public-health use cases still need bounded authority and post-event review.

Data sensitivity increases across the pipeline

The same measurement can become more consequential when it is linked, interpreted or acted upon.

Raw observation

Example: a pollutant reading or a single motion sensor sample. Sensitivity depends on what was measured and where.

Persistent pattern

Example: repeated time-stamped movement or physiological readings. Patterns can reveal routines or conditions even without a name.

Identity-linked record

Example: a pattern joined to an account, device owner, address or reference database. This creates a direct privacy and access-control boundary.

Inference and intervention

Example: a model labels a person, animal or place as risky and triggers an action. Errors can now affect rights, services, movement or reputation.

Minimum governance questions: What is the specific purpose? What is the least data needed? Who is the data subject or responsible steward? Is collection authorized? Who can access raw data, derived features and identity keys? Can the person or community know, correct or challenge the record? What is the retention period? How are security incidents, model errors and secondary uses audited?
Key distinction: NIST IR 8259 Rev. 1 is manufacturer guidance for improving IoT product cybersecurity across the product lifecycle. It does not certify a particular sensor network as safe, lawful or privacy-preserving. Technical security, privacy protection and legitimate authority are three separate requirements.
