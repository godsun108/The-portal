# SEB-001-G0-001 — Feedstock Sampling and Assay Plan

**Revision:** 0.1 | **Date:** 2026-11-26 | **Status:** Proposed protocol for qualified laboratory review  
**Linked basis:** DB-001, MB-001, PFD-001 | **Gate:** G0 — feedstock evidence

## 1. Objective and decision use
Establish a traceable, representative description of the incoming whole hemp stalk and its separated bast, hurd and fines fractions. Data will support decortication selection, process route screening, mass-balance inputs, storage design and preliminary product allocation. A literature composition value is not a substitute for measurements on project feedstock.

## 2. Sampling population and strata
Treat each supplier, cultivar/variety, field or growing region, harvest date, storage condition and physical fraction as a distinct stratum unless equivalence is demonstrated. Never pool materially different feedstocks before documenting their identity. Sample:
- Whole stalk as received.
- Whole stalk after moisture conditioning, if applicable.
- Bast, hurd and fines from a documented decortication trial.
- Any process residue proposed for thermal conversion, as a separate material.
Record supplier/lot ID, legal chain-of-custody documents, cultivar if known, field/region, harvest date, storage history, bale/container ID, receipt date, wet mass, visible condition and sampling operator.

## 3. Initial screening campaign — proposed minimum
For initial characterization, target **three independently sourced or independently harvested lots** representing the feedstock envelope intended for Stage 1. From each lot, collect at least **10 spatially distributed increments** across different bales/locations/depths, avoiding selection by appearance. Combine increments only within that lot and material stratum to form a representative composite. Prepare a sealed archive split and a laboratory split using a documented riffle/quartering or laboratory-approved procedure. Record sample mass, container, seal, storage temperature/humidity where relevant, transfer and chain of custody.

This is a project screening protocol, not a statutory sampling standard. The laboratory must review sample size, preservation, homogenization and method suitability before collection. If the lot is visibly heterogeneous, increase increments and analyze selected increments individually to quantify variability.

## 4. Required analyses
| Property | Why it matters | Reporting requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture, wet basis | Receipt mass correction, drying/storage | Method, replicate, uncertainty |
| Ash | Inert load, fouling, product contamination | Dry basis |
| Structural carbohydrates: glucan/cellulose proxy and xylan/hemicellulose proxy | Product potential and component balance | Dry basis; method and hydrolysis correction |
| Acid-insoluble and acid-soluble lignin | Lignin balance and product quality | Dry basis; method |
| Extractives, water- and solvent-soluble | Pretreatment load and losses | Dry basis; method |
| Protein / nitrogen | Composition and possible contaminants | Dry basis |
| Metals and mineral elements | Ash, catalyst/process compatibility, product suitability | Element list, detection limits |
| Chloride / sulfur (where relevant) | Corrosion, emissions and wastewater | Method and detection limits |
| Bulk density | Receiving/storage/conveyor sizing | As-received and conditioned basis |
| Particle-size distribution | Size reduction, extraction and filtration | Sieve/image method and mass fractions |
| Bast/hurd/fines split | Decortication allocation | Dry mass basis; trial conditions |
| Fiber length / damage (bast) | Fiber/pulp and composite qualification | Method and distribution |
| Contaminants / foreign matter | Equipment damage and product quality | Type and mass fraction |

Use qualified laboratories and recognized validated methods appropriate to lignocellulosic biomass (for example, applicable NREL biomass analytical procedures where suitable). The laboratory shall identify exact method numbers/versions, sample preparation, calibration, detection/quantification limits, uncertainty and deviations. This document does not prescribe a chemical test recipe.

## 5. Decortication trial protocol
A trial shall record incoming lot ID, wet and dry mass, moisture, machine identity/configuration, operating settings supplied by the vendor, run duration, throughput, energy measurement, downtime, and recovered mass of bast, hurd, fines, dust and unaccounted loss. Sample each output independently. Calculate dry-basis split:
- Bast fraction = dry bast mass / dry feed mass.
- Hurd fraction = dry hurd mass / dry feed mass.
- Fines fraction = dry fines mass / dry feed mass.
- Unaccounted fraction = 1 − sum(measured output dry fractions).
Report fiber quality and contamination as well as mass split; maximizing bast mass alone is not an acceptance criterion.

## 6. QA/QC and acceptance
- Each result is traceable to lot, sample, laboratory, method and report.
- At least one blind duplicate per sampling campaign is requested; lab to recommend additional QC.
- Duplicate precision limits are method-specific and must be agreed with the lab before testing; do not impose one universal RPD limit across all analytes.
- Moisture basis, dry basis, as-received basis and any ash-free basis are explicit.
- Mass split from decortication is reconciled; target 95–105% dry mass closure for a controlled trial, with deviations investigated and documented.
- Report between-lot variability; do not use a single pooled mean without range/standard deviation.
- Missing or below-detection values are not silently set to zero.

## 7. G0 exit criteria
G0 is complete when (a) three representative lots are characterized or documented reasons for fewer lots are approved; (b) whole-stalk moisture/composition data are received; (c) decortication split and fiber quality are measured; (d) chain of custody and lab methods are traceable; (e) feedstock supply volume, seasonality, legal compliance and delivered-cost evidence are documented; and (f) MB-001 inputs are updated with source IDs and uncertainty.

## 8. Hold points
No chemical pretreatment trial, thermal conversion, graphene upgrading, purchase of machinery, paid laboratory work, sample shipment or binding supplier commitment is authorized by this plan. The owner must approve any spend, shipment or contract. Any physical testing must be conducted by qualified facilities under their approved safety and environmental procedures.
