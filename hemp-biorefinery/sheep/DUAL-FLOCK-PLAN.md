# Two-Flock Sheep Program — Wool/Textiles + Meat/Grazing
Decision confirmed 2026-10-12: **pursue BOTH** a wool-producing sheep line and a meat/grazing sheep line, subject to appropriate land, animal care, finances and feed permissions. Research stage only; no animals or spending authorized.

## Two complementary genetic and commercial tracks
**Wool and textiles flock:** investigate heat-adapted wool producers, especially Florida Cracker / Florida Native sheep (dual-purpose local heritage breed producing wool, with breed conservation questions), and compare additional breeds only after veterinarian, fiber-processing and Florida-climate assessment. Wool traits: micron/fineness, staple length, annual clean fleece yield, break strength, comfort, shearing/cleaning cost and textile customer demand. The fleece does not automatically become garment-grade yarn.

**Meat and grazing flock:** compare Katahdin, St. Croix, Florida Cracker, Dorper and St. Augustine breed/lines for parasite resistance, heat tolerance, mothering, growth and local buyer needs. Hair sheep generally do not produce a marketable wool clip, so manage them as a distinct revenue stream. A wool sheep might also be dual-purpose; don't promise additive meat and wool profit until economics are measured.

**Shared infrastructure if suitable:** pasture/rotational paddocks, water, shade/shelter, predator proof fences, veterinarian and general handling facilities; breeding and flock records and parasite management must be breed- and herd-appropriate. Separate groups when required for breeding control, nutrition, disease, lambing or animal welfare. No universal stocking rate before local carrying capacity assessment.

## Florida candidate grounded in extension
UF/IFAS 'Selection of Sheep Meat Breeds in Florida' identifies Florida Cracker/Florida Native sheep as adapted to humid conditions and produces **wool** while used for meat; Katahdin, St. Croix and some other hair-sheep types are meat-oriented. Local parasite burden (especially Haemonchus contortus) is a critical breed and management consideration.
Source: https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/VM264
UF/IFAS hair breeds: https://sfyl.ifas.ufl.edu/duval/agriculture-and-agribusiness-management/livestock-and-poultry/sheep/sheep-breeds/

## Development gates
0. **No-spend market survey:** two customer groups: local wool buyers/processors and meat buyers; evaluate actual processing costs and demand.
1. **Breed selection:** score each candidate on welfare/climate/parasites and actual fleece and carcass data, accessibility and conservation status; select distinct or compatible flocks.
2. **Site and veterinary plan:** secured lawful land, adequate pasture/water, mineral plans for sheep, experienced daily caretaker, disease/biosecurity plan, shearing services, predator/flood/heat contingencies.
3. **Cash model:** upfront fences/animal acquisition/handling and ongoing feeds/hay, lambing, shearing, vet, transport, product processing, insurance and emergency reserves. Keep economic ledgers separate.
4. **Acquire only after explicit approval:** small initial flock size determined by professional stocking assessment and ability to care for all animals; conventional nutrition only.
5. **Wool chain:** verified fleece -> professional washing/scouring -> spinning/weaving/felting partner -> measured textile quality -> qualified sale or community distribution.
6. **Meat chain:** responsible breeding/animal welfare -> applicable inspected processing and sales laws -> actual customer purchases.
7. **Hemp feed R&D (independent gate):** exact sheep-eligible feed ingredient and use must be cleared by competent regulators and veterinarian. No hemp rations, inclusion levels or hemp-fed marketing without legal/residue safety evidence.
