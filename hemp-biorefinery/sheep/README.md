# Regenerative Sheep and Hemp Feed Research Program
Created 2026-10-12. Research only: no animals acquired, hemp fed, experimental feeding, expenditure or wool sale authorized.

## Confirmed direction: BOTH wool and meat/grazing sheep
We will research a dual-flock (or qualified dual-purpose) model for **wool/textiles** and **meat/regenerative grazing**. The wool and meat operations have separate product economics, animal records and breed-selection criteria, while potentially sharing suitable infrastructure. [See the two-flock roadmap](DUAL-FLOCK-PLAN.md).

## Mission
Build a healthy and financially viable sheep operation aligned with regenerative grazing and our **community textile program**. Evaluate breeds for wool, meat, dairy or conservation grazing separately. Investigate whether any particular hemp-derived ingredients become legally permitted and safe in sheep feed; until then rely on species-appropriate conventional feed approved by the flock's veterinarian/nutritionist.

## The four possible value streams
- **Wool / fleece:** shear sheep humanely, grade fleece and partner with professional scouring, carding, spinning, weaving and felt makers; research dyeing, garments, blankets, insulation and other qualified products. Hair-sheep breeds will not yield comparable textile fleece.
- **Regenerative grazing:** manage pastures using measured ground cover, carrying capacity, soil health, water availability and parasite pressure. Sheep and cattle can share a larger farm under an expert-designed grazing system; do not assume mixed grazing automatically reduces parasite problems.
- **Livestock products:** if eventually pursued, compliant meat/dairy channels with veterinary care, inspected processing and animal-product regulations, not an assumed revenue stream.
- **Farm nutrients:** manure and eligible bedding for controlled composting, with pathogen and nutrient management before land use; never route industrial chemical/pyrolysis/fungal remediation residues into feed.

## Critical feed law gate
A hemp plant being legally cultivated is NOT equivalent to hemp seed cake, meal, stalk, leaf, flower or extraction residue being authorized as feed. Animal-food ingredient eligibility is **species and intended-use specific**, and research feeding sheep does not amount to U.S. feed authorization. No currently confirmed sheep-specific hemp feed approval in these sources. Contact FDA CVM and Florida feed authorities for written status of each exact proposed ingredient before any ration or sale of hemp-fed animal products.
- FDA animal feed ingredients: https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-foods-feeds/animal-food-ingredients
- FDA hemp animal-food FAQ: https://www.fda.gov/news-events/public-health-focus/fda-regulation-cannabis-and-cannabis-derived-products-including-cannabidiol-cbd

## Why the caution is real
A controlled hemp-stubble experiment detected cannabinoids in sheep meat and fat: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8714185/
Another study found THC in subcutaneous fat even after hemp biomass feeding stopped, including at 140 days in one sheep: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9661295/
These are experimental data (from Australia), NOT permission or a sufficient basis for a safe feed dosage or withholding period in U.S. sheep.

## Humane flock acquisition trajectory
**Stage 0 — Research now:** choose wool-oriented vs meat/hair vs dual-purpose goal. Check suitable climate and breeds, wet-season parasite management, Florida zoning, land and owner schedule, buyer demand and wool-processing partners. Separate sheep feed regulatory memo from cattle memo.
**Stage 1 — Qualified farm plan:** land and water rights, fencing secure for sheep, predator management, shade and shelter, rotational paddocks, safe handling, quarantine/biosecurity, shearing access, qualified veterinarian, forage/hay contingency and working capital.
**Stage 2 — Budget and flock sizing:** no fixed animal number or stocking-rate assumption. Derive from local pasture carrying capacity, experienced stockperson and extension advice; calculate costs and reserve. Include breeding/lambing and flock-health needs as applicable.
**Stage 3 — Conventional-feed flock:** only after care infrastructure, legal permits and explicit capital approval. Species-appropriate forage, minerals and supplemental feed overseen by qualified animal nutritionist; sheep are particularly sensitive to excessive copper. Log health, weight/condition, parasite measures, expenses and wool quality.
**Stage 4 — Wool circular supply chain:** grade fleeces, evaluate washable yield, fiber diameter, staple length, textile processing price and actual community customer interest; develop textile product prototypes through qualified external vendors.
**Stage 5 — Hemp-feed approval inquiry:** select exact hemp ingredient (seed/meal/oil/stalk/leaf are not interchangeable), request written feed status from regulators and pursue qualified veterinary/university research partner if justified.
**Stage 6 — Regulated feeding research only if independently approved:** researcher/institution/vet-designed trial, animal care/food-residue requirements and consumer safety; no do-it-yourself feeding or inferred safe schedule.
**Stage 7 — Commercial integration only after proof:** lawful feed ingredient, reliable evidence of safe animal-product residues, accurate labeling and commercial economics.

## Operating calendar — future care, NOT hemp feeding dosage
- **Daily:** check each animal's appetite, breathing, gait, general health, water and legally appropriate forage/feed; secure fences, shelter/shade and predator controls. Follow actual veterinarian-approved welfare schedule.
- **Weekly:** pasture and fencing inspection, hay/mineral inventory, condition scoring observations and parasite surveillance; plan moves based on pasture growth, not a universal number of days.
- **Monthly:** reconcile actual lambing/growth/wool/expenses, vet consult if indicated, adjust pasture plan.
- **Seasonal:** shearing schedule appropriate to breed/climate, pre-lambing nutrition and birth response plan, hurricane and heat/storm readiness, forage and parasite contingency, soil sampling and breeding planning.
- **Annual:** land capacity, welfare and economic audit, wool customer demand, soil improvements and species-specific feed approval recheck.

## Florida-specific considerations
UF/IFAS advises sheep-appropriate minerals because copper tolerance is lower than many livestock species. Gastrointestinal parasites, especially barber pole worms, need carefully managed grazing and veterinary programs. Florida extension's example average stocking figures are **not** safe planning numbers for unknown land.
